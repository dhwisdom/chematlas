A Lewis structure is a map of connectivity. Geometry is the moment chemistry becomes spatial—and properties begin to emerge.

04.1

A polar bond is not yet a polar molecule.

When two atoms share electrons unequally, the bond carries a dipole. But molecular behavior depends on what all of those bond dipoles do together. Geometry determines whether they reinforce one another or cancel.

This is why carbon dioxide and water can contain polar bonds yet behave very differently as complete molecules.

VECTOR MODELμ⃗molecule = Σ μ⃗bond
04.2

Shape decides what survives.

In linear CO₂, the two C=O bond dipoles point in opposite directions with equal magnitude, so their vector sum is zero. In bent H₂O, the O–H dipoles do not oppose each other directly, leaving a net molecular dipole.

The important idea is not to memorize which molecules are polar. It is to reason from bond polarity + geometry.

WORKED EXAMPLE · CO₂ VS H₂O
1

Identify polar bonds. Both C=O and O–H bonds have meaningful electronegativity differences.

2

Determine geometry. CO₂ is linear; H₂O is bent because oxygen has two lone pairs.

3

Add the bond dipoles as vectors. Linear symmetry cancels in CO₂; the bent geometry leaves a net dipole in H₂O.
